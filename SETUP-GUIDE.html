Loan Manager: put it online with a login

This guide has 3 parts. Part 1 and 2 are done once, on a computer. Part 3 is how you use it every day, on phones.

What you need: a computer with internet, a Google account (Gmail), and the 4 files in this folder: index.html, firebase-config.js, firestore.rules and this guide. Screens on the Firebase website change sometimes, so a button may look a little different. Look for the same words.

Part 1: Set up Firebase (free)

  1. Open Firebase. Go to console.firebase.google.com and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Make a NEW project. Click Create a project (or Add project). Name it community-finance. Click Continue. Turn off Google Analytics. Click Create project, wait, then click Continue.
    Always use a new project for this app. If you already have a Firebase project for another app, do not use it. The rules in step 5 would replace that app's rules and could break it.
  3. Turn on login. On the left, click Build, then Authentication, then Get started. Click Email/Password. Switch the first switch on. Click Save.
  4. Make the database. On the left, click Build, then Firestore Database, then Create database. If it asks for an edition, choose Standard. Choose the location nearest to you (you cannot change it later). Choose Start in production mode. Click Create.
  5. Paste the security rules. This step protects your clients' data.
    1. On your computer, right-click firestore.rules, choose Open with, then Notepad.
    2. Find the text CHANGE-THIS-CODE (near the middle). Replace it with a secret word only you know, for example blue-lion-2026. Keep the quotation marks around it. Write this code down: you need it in step 9.
    3. Click inside the Notepad text. Press Ctrl + A (selects everything), then Ctrl + C (copies).
    4. In Firebase, on the Firestore Database page, click the Rules tab at the top. A box with some text appears.
    5. Click inside that box. Press Ctrl + A then press Delete (the box is now empty). Press Ctrl + V (pastes your rules).
    6. Click Publish. Wait for the message that the rules were published.
  6. Connect the app. Click the gear next to Project Overview, then Project settings. Scroll to Your apps and click the web symbol </>. Type the nickname loans. Do not tick Firebase Hosting. Click Register app. A box appears with a block of text called firebaseConfig. Keep this page open.
  7. Fill in the settings file. Open firebase-config.js in Notepad. Delete the 6 lines that start with apiKey, authDomain and so on. In their place, paste the 6 lines from Firebase (the lines between { and }). The first line of the file must still say export const firebaseConfig = { and the last line must still be };. Save the file.

Part 2: Put the app online (free)

  1. Upload the folder. Go to app.netlify.com/drop and sign up (free). Drag the whole loan-cloud folder onto the page. Wait. Netlify gives you a web address ending in netlify.app. Open it: you should see the login screen. (Do not double-click index.html on your computer. It only works from the web address.)
    Changed a file later? Drag the folder onto Netlify again. Your data is not lost, because it lives in Firebase.
  2. Become the owner (first time only). On the login screen tap Create an account with your email and a password. You will see "waiting for the owner to approve". Under First time setting up, type your secret code from step 5 and tap Become the owner. You are in.
  3. Set up your loan products. Go to More, tap Add starter loan products, then Loan products and rates, and change the rates to your own.

Part 3: Every day

  1. Add staff. Give your staff the web address. They tap Create an account and wait. You open More, then Staff and access, and tap Officer beside their email. Officers can add clients, make loan applications and record repayments. Only you (the owner) can approve loans, change rates, write off or delete.
  2. Add the app to the phone's home screen. In Chrome, tap the three dots, then Add to Home screen.
  3. Weak internet. After you have logged in, the app keeps working when the signal drops. Entries are saved on the phone and sent when the internet returns. Log in once with internet first.
  4. Backup. Each week, owner taps More, Copy backup of all data and pastes it into a note or WhatsApp to yourself.

Test it before real use

  1. Create a second account (use another email), approve it as Officer, and log in with it on another phone.
  2. Open a Pending loan as the officer. You must see "Waiting for the owner to approve" and no Approve button.
  3. Open the web address in a private window without logging in. You must see only the login screen.

If something goes wrong

  • "Firebase is not connected yet": the settings in firebase-config.js were not saved or uploaded. Repeat steps 7 and 8.
  • "You are not allowed to do that": the rules were not published, or you are using an officer account for an owner job. Repeat step 5.
  • "That setup code is wrong, or an owner already exists": type the exact secret word you put in the rules (capitals and dashes matter). It works only once.
  • Login says unauthorized domain: in Firebase open Authentication, then Settings, then Authorized domains, and add your netlify.app address.
  • "Quota exceeded": the free Firebase plan has a daily limit. It suits a small lender. If you outgrow it, Firebase offers a pay-as-you-go plan.
Privacy: you are keeping your clients' personal details online. Use strong passwords, approve only people you trust, and check what Uganda's data protection law requires of a lender. I am not a lawyer.
